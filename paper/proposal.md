## Summary

The main goal of this project is to rewrite the current Frequnecy Resolved Optical Gating (FROG) MATLAB script(s) in Python. In doing so, this will allow my lab much more usability, and the potential for much more usability of the files obtained from the spectrometer in the FROG setup. A chirped light pulse is a pulse that makes certain colors travel faster than others, ie. light of different frequencies arrive at different times relative to some detection device. The importance of this is after sending a collimated beam through a nonlinear crystal relative to the beams focus after traveling through a lens, we can chirp the light pulse(s), which spreads the frequencies out, ie. increases the bandwidth. Utilizing the FROG setup, we can then detect the bandwidth relative to the original and determine how much the beams bandwidth increased, which in turn lets us know the minimum length in time this light pulse can be. Overall, the FROG setup is a device that measures how much we've increased the bandwidth which then tells us how short we can make laser pulses in time. In Terahertz-Scanning Tunneling Microscopy (THz-STM), we rely on short pulses of light (In the THz frequency regime) to drive electrons across a tunnel junction (For us that includes the tungsten (W) STM tip (The probe), the gap separating the tip and the sample, and the sample itself). The shorter we can make these pulses, the better we can resolve the atomic structure of these samples in time. Overall, after the beam goes through the FROG setup and hits the spectrometer, we are given a few .M files with the main one giving us intensity vs. time of the beam. From here we get some very important results, with the main two being intensity vs. time and wavelength, as well as the relative phase vs. wavelength which can tell us a ton about our limitations when it comes to the individual pulses. 

## Overview

I am in condensed matter physics, specifically THz-STM. In conventional STM, we rely on a voltage to make electrons quantum mechanically tunnel across the tunnel junction, the main difference is in THz-STM, we can improve our temporal resolution by using a laser pulse to act as said voltage, the shorter it is the better off our temporal resolution will be, ie. we can essentially see things that move quicker in a clearer way. This work matters because we study conductive materials and improving the temporal resolution allows us to get a better idea of what defects are within said materials, therefore changing properties of said material such as lattice shape and or overall style, conductivity, and etc. Computation software supports this project because the light pulses are an important factor and the shorter we can make them, the better our system wil be. The only way to analyze the pulses is to use a script to read in the files, and through quite a bit of mathematics, we can then get out important aspects that we want such as the intensity vs. time and wavelength, as well as relative phase vs. wavelength.
